Why each number is that number

No price on this site was invented, and none of them is positioning. Three of the six are arithmetic and the workings are here.

The numberWhy it is that number
£10 — What did I actually grant it?The floor is arithmetic, not positioning. At £5 the fixed card fee alone is about four per cent of the transaction
£50 to £100 — Which parts of the regulation actually reach me?A band, not a number. Where a case lands inside it depends on how much of the graph the situation touches
£150 to £1,000 — Will somebody look at my situation with me?The width is deliberate. It is a person's time, and the range covers an hour at one end and a working week at the other
£5,000 to £10,000 — What would a team of security professionals find?Above about £1,000 a card stops making sense — at £10,000 the card fee alone reaches roughly £250 — so the engagement goes by invoice and bank transfer, or through the cloud marketplace after a conversation. The £500 deposit is below that threshold, so the deposit is a payment link and the balance is not
By depth band — Can I have the same result in a form the board will read?Priced against the depth band of the offer it attaches to, because the work is the rendering and the rendering scales with what there is to render
By depth band — Will somebody put their name to it?Priced against the depth band of the offer it attaches to

The floor is the clearest of them: at £5 the fixed card fee alone is about four per cent of the transaction, so £10 is the floor and £5 is not a cheaper version of it — it is a worse one. At the other end, at £10,000 a card costs up to about £250, which is why tier 4 does not take one. projected 10 Sep 2026

The offer identifier is the stable part

A printed code redirects to /d/<id>/ and nothing else about it is load-bearing. Not the host, not the path above it, not a word of the copy.

That is deliberate, and it is the one piece of engineering this site does purely because of a disagreement. A ruling of 10 September placed a storefront under the risk product rather than on the platform domain. The instruction in hand overrode it and named this host. The ruling stands on the record, unedited, and the build treats the host as moveable: every internal link here is relative, the host appears in no sentence of copy, and the identifiers above do not change. If the site moves, it costs a DNS record and no reprinting. read, not run 10 Sep 2026

How each one is paid for

A standing payment link carries exactly one price, and two offers on this page have one. Tier 1 is £10 and could hold a link tomorrow. Tiers 2 and 3 are bands, so their link is issued once the band is fixed for the case. Tier 4 carries two amounts: the engagement is above the threshold where a card makes sense and is invoiced, while its £500 deposit is below it and is a standing link. The two add-ons are priced against the depth band of whatever they attach to. That is arithmetic about how a fixed-price link works, not a policy about who may pay. read, not run 11 Sep 2026 projected 11 Sep 2026

No link has been created for any of them. Every offer carries an empty checkout field, so every card above shows its code and its delivery page rather than a button, and this page says which of the five reasons applies to each. Issuing one is a single pasted line, and the build holds whatever lands there to the payment provider's own checkout hosts over HTTPS. does not exist yet 11 Sep 2026 How the rails work, and why they never meet.
