*2026-09-16 · Voice memo, transcribed*

## What we read in it

### <b>The correction that changes the most: “I’ve done it.”</b>

“Every workflow I’m describing, I’ve done it… when I create the vaults for the examples, I’ve done those policies, and I’ve done consulting with other projects.” The store has been saying something technically true and practically false. Its claims say the £500 and £1,500 levels have never run for a paying buyer — accurate, and what a reader takes from it is that nobody has ever done this work at all. Those are different sentences and only one of them is true.

### What replaces it is stronger than what it removes

The evidence was never missing; it was on a different domain. sgit.ai publishes vault after vault of exactly this work — risk graphs, the regulation graph as a citable object, a conformance layer, a penetration test delivered as a vault, the licence-to-operate model — each with a read key anybody can open right now. “Here are twenty of these, open one” beats both “nobody has done this” and a testimonial.

### The distinction to keep, stated once and plainly

The work has been done. This store has not sold it. That second sentence stays, because a first buyer really is the first through this checkout and that is worth them knowing — but it stops being the headline and stops standing in for the first.

### A name on the delivery

“Look, I, Dinis, am the one delivering this. I have 20 years of cybersecurity.” Same ask as the reviewer memo, arriving from the selling side rather than the trust side: the two upper levels are somebody’s work and the store has never said whose.

### The homepage is a shop front, not an essay

“Big banners, buy now, the products up front.” And everything about sgit, the mechanism and the meta moves to the admin side. The home page today opens by explaining what an Agent Behaviour Policy is; a shop opens with what is for sale.

### <b>The real design brief is a physical one</b>

“Laptop open, somebody comes along — who are you? A founder. Boom. And you want to buy this. Boom. Click, click, navigate, select, touch, go.” That is not a web page, it is a counter at an event, operated by a person standing next to the buyer. It sets the whole shape: big targets, few of them, works on a tablet, no dead ends, and a discount code applied by following a link rather than by typing.

### Delivery times, given as numbers rather than as a principle

£10 immediately. The vault in one to two days. The customised review in one to three days from receiving the customisation, which is the clock that actually matters. The top level in one to five days, depending on load. And an honest hedge that is also good copy: “it would be a lovely problem to have if we had to add stock.”

### And the process is set: catalogue, plan, execute in sequence, push often

“Catalogue them really carefully, map exactly what I’m saying, create the action plan, then execute them all — bit by bit, and keep pushing to prod.” Some changes warrant a major version rather than a minor one.

## What this does not decide

### <b>“Remove all the stuff that we have never done this before” — read as a fix, not a delete</b>

Taken literally this deletes the disclosure apparatus, which is the single thing two independent reviewers praised without being asked and the reason a founder persona trusted the site at all. Read as the complaint it is, the problem is not that the site discloses — it is that the disclosure is doing the job of the evidence, because the evidence lives on another domain and was never linked. So: the evidence leads, the disclosure narrows to what is actually undone (a sale through this checkout), and nothing that is true gets removed. If the intent was the literal one, say so and it is one edit.

### Which claims are simply wrong now, and which were always right

Every claim has to be re-read one at a time against “the work has been done.” Some are wrong as written. Some are precisely right and were misread. Doing this by search-and-replace would break the ledger, which is the one artefact on this site that must not be edited carelessly.

### Twenty years, and what is citable

The claim is the project lead’s to make and the site should carry it in their voice with a source, not assert it in the shop’s voice. Same rule as the reviewer page: nothing goes up that is not traceable to something published.

### Testimonials

Named in the memo as something the homepage should have. There are none. The review pages are the closest thing and they are critiques, which is a different object. A placeholder that says it is empty is the house style; an invented one would undo everything else in this memo.

### What “depending on inventory” means for a thing with no stock

Good copy and it needs a true referent. The real constraint is one person’s calendar, which is worth saying in those words rather than borrowing a warehouse metaphor that would be the only false sentence on the page.
