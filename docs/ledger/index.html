Pages cite a claim rather than restating it, so the same fact cannot be strong on one page and hedged on another. The rightmost column is joined at build time: it shows where each claim is actually said, and it is not editable by hand.

Why a store publishes one of these

Because checkability is the thing being sold. An offer page that cannot be checked is arguing against the product sitting beside it.

And because the alternative is worse in a specific, predictable way: without a ledger, the sentence "the pipeline does not exist" survives one draft, gets softened to "the pipeline is in development" in the next, and is gone by the third. Here it is a row with a state, cited from the page that makes the claim, and the build fails if a page cites a claim that is not in this table.

Every agent needs a licence to operate. This is where you buy one. Six offers, four of them with a price and a code, two of them listed and not yet buyable, grouped by which of three buyers each was built for. The paid thing is not access and it is not customisation. It is independence.

Nothing here is a compliance assessment, nothing here is a mark of conformity to any standard, and no opinion here is personal: the company issues every opinion, and the person who sells is never the person who signs. Outputs are model generated and marked as such.
