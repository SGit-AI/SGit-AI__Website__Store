## What is actually in one

Four objects, and the order they come in matters.

- **The mandate** — what the agent is authorised and expected to do. **Elicited**, because you already know it.
- **The grant** — everything it can actually do. **Measured**, from the deployment shape, the account and the credentials. It is almost never enumerated anywhere, and it is reliably larger than whoever deployed it expected.
- **The delta** — permitted, and nobody asked for it. **Derived**, and recomputed whenever either input moves.
- **The barrier** — what stands between the agent and each capability. Four kinds, and **only one of them is a control**: a setting the agent's own account can flip is not one, because the grant includes the ability to remove it.

**A grant on its own is an inventory, and nobody acts on an inventory.** *Your agent
can do sixteen things* is a shrug. *Your agent can do sixteen things and you
authorised five* is a finding.

## There is no score here, and there will not be one

**The same policy is dangerous in one deployment and harmless in another, and
nothing about the document changed.** The most permissive grant imaginable, running
where there is nothing to reach, is a low risk; the same grant with a production
database attached tomorrow is a different one. Risk is a function of the behaviour policy,
the assets, the consequences and the date — **and the behaviour policy is the input that does
not move.**

So there is no rating on anything here, no traffic light and no risk level. **A
policy cannot be dangerous. A deployment can.**

## The free thing, and the paid thing

**The templates are free and public**, with published read keys, at
riskmandate.ai. Go and read one before you buy anything here. That is not a
generosity and it is not a funnel: a policy nobody can check is a policy asking to
be trusted, and the whole point of this document type is that it can be checked.

**What is priced here is an instance.** The same template with the mandate
corrected, your name on the licence and no public key on it — and at the upper two
levels, the correction done with you rather than by you. **The paid thing is not
access and it is not customisation. It is independence:** at every level more of
the result is yours and less of it depends on us still being here.

## What this site will not tell you

Nothing here is a compliance assessment, and nothing here is a mark of conformity
to a standard. Neither describes this, and that language appears on no deliverable
and on no page. **The company issues every opinion**, with an express
non-assumption of personal responsibility on its face, and **the person who sells
is never the person who signs.**

Outputs are model generated and marked as such {{claim:transparency-article}}.
Findings are triaged, never raw: recall-optimised agents run at 0.388 precision,
which is about three findings in five wrong, so **every finding that reaches you
has been reproduced rather than reviewed** {{claim:precision-0388}}.

And one sentence that a reader might expect on a page like this **is not here on
purpose**: we do not tell you what we can and cannot read, because six questions
about what leaks have not been answered yet. [The disclosures page says which
six](/disclosures/). {{claim:cannot-read-unanswered}}
