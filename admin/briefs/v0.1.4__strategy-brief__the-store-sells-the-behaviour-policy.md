# The Store Sells The Behaviour Policy: The Four Tiers Do Not Move, And The Lab Was Built On A Vocabulary That Was Already Published

**version** v0.1.4 · **date** 11 September 2026 · **from** the agent building store.sgit.ai · **to** whoever rules on the offer page, whoever rebuilds `/lab/`, and whoever stands at the table in Lisbon in six days

**type** Strategy brief for store.sgit.ai, written against abp.sgit.ai v0.2.0 and the voice memo of 11 September

---

## What This Is

The store has been built for five days against a pack that named four tiers and never named the thing behind them, and as of today that thing exists, is published, and is better specified than anything in this repository: **an Agent Behaviour Policy is the grant, the mandate, the delta and the barrier, for one agent in one deployment, with no score.** The finding of this brief is that **no price on this site changes and no offer is added** — the ABP is the name for what tiers one and three already deliver, which is what the product brief on abp.sgit.ai says in its own ladder table — but that almost everything *around* the prices does change: tier one stops being a questions page waiting on an application built in August and becomes a deliverable draft ABP whose marginal cost is one line and a build; `/lab/` stops inventing twelve grants and nine mandates and reads the twenty-three published capability primitives instead; the configurator stops asking a buyer to fill in a blank and starts handing them a draft that is **conservative on purpose**, because the correction is the elicitation and it needs no access, no data and no packet sent anywhere; the red on the excess column comes out, because a traffic light is a verdict and the ABP carries none; the barrier column goes in, because **unbounded excess is the only number on the label a buyer can move** and the gap between excess and unbounded excess is the business case for a control; tier two's regulatory citation changes, because the European deployer obligations were deferred to 2 December 2027 and the reason a United Kingdom buyer will actually cite is consumer guidance published on 9 March 2026; a fourth buyer appears with no new offer behind it, being the security vendor who wants an ABP run twice to show what its own control moves; and one word that the whole demand story rests on may never appear on a single byte of this site, which is a ruling and not an edit.

**New in this brief, beyond what abp.sgit.ai already decided:** the unit-economics reading that makes tier one shippable this week; the six findings against what this repository has already built, four of which are defects I introduced; the fourth buyer; the four new build checks; and the cut line for six days.

## The One-Line Version

> **Tier one was always a draft Agent Behaviour Policy. Nobody had named it, so it was priced at ten pounds and described as a questions page waiting on a lost application. The name is the product, the data to produce it is published, and the thing blocking it turns out not to be blocking it.**

## Finding 1 — The ABP maps onto the existing ladder and moves no number

From the product strategy brief on abp.sgit.ai, which places the ABP on this site's own four tiers and changes nothing:

| Tier | Price on this site today | What it is, named | Changes to the price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | £10 | **A draft ABP.** Derived from a deployment shape, handed over, corrected by the buyer | none |
| **2** | £50 to £100 | **Not an ABP.** The regulation an ABP is later mapped against | none |
| **3** | £150 to £1,000 | **The full ABP.** Mandate elicited properly, grant measured against the real deployment, each prohibition marked with where it is enforced, delivered as a vault | none |
| **4** | £5,000 to £10,000 | **The ABP, plus the assessment of whether the prohibitions are actually enforced where they claim to be** | none |

This matters more than it reads. `tools/check_site.py` freezes all six prices against the pack and fails the release if one moves, and the pack's first rule about what may not be invented is a price. **A product that arrives five days before an event and requires no price to move is the only kind that can be adopted in five days.**

The two add-ons are unaffected: the multi-format add-on renders an ABP for a reader who will not open a vault, and the signed-opinion add-on is the signature on the tier above — and neither can be bought, for the reasons already in the ledger.

## Finding 2 — Tier one is shippable this week, and its blocker is not blocking it

The ledger currently says tier one's application **was built in August and has not been located**, and that until it is found, tier one is a questions page. That claim stays true. **What changes is that it no longer decides whether tier one has a product.**

abp.sgit.ai instrumented what its five worked examples cost to produce, because — in its own words — *nobody knows what an ABP costs to produce, and the store has to price one*:

- Five worked ABPs, **five minutes each**, **nought questions asked of a human**, about four hours in total with essentially all of it spent on the generator, the promoted schema and the provenance line.
- **The marginal cost of the sixth, for a shape already in the published map, is one line in a list and a build.**
- The number nobody has is what it costs for a shape **not** in the map, where the grant has to be measured rather than looked up. That one has never been done.

Read that against the ladder and the pricing story stops being a defence and becomes an argument:

| | What it needs | What it costs to make | Why that price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1, £10** | A shape already in the published map | One line and a build | The floor is the card fee, not the work. This is the tier the arithmetic was always about |
| **Tier 3, £150 to £1,000** | A shape **not** in the map, grant measured | **Unknown — never done once** | The band is wide because the cost is unmeasured, and the page can now say exactly that instead of saying "it is a person's time" |

**Recommendation.** Restate tier one on the offer page and its delivery page as *a draft Agent Behaviour Policy for your deployment shape*, keep `t1-app-not-located` in the ledger unchanged and unsoftened, and add one claim saying what the draft does **not** depend on. The honest sentence is that the draft is derived from published data and the measured version is not the same thing — which is the distinction between tier one and tier three anyway.

## Finding 3 — `/lab/` invented a vocabulary that was already published, with provenance

This is the largest defect in what I built, and it is mine. `data/brief.yml` carries **12 grants, 9 mandates and 10 surfaces that I wrote**, plus a `justified_by` edge set I invented to make a delta computable. abp.sgit.ai publishes, at a stable address, with cross-origin access, under CC BY 4.0, pinned to a version:

| | Published | In `data/brief.yml` today |
|---|---|---|
| Capability primitives | **23**, as `verb.object.reach`, each with a reach and an undo class | 12 "grants" I wrote |
| Barriers | **4** kinds, with the enforcer test | **none — the concept is absent entirely** |
| Undo classes | **3** | none |
| Deployment shapes (profiles) | **9** | 10 "surfaces" I wrote |
| Mandates | **8** | 9 I wrote |
| Deltas | **9**, derived, each pinning the version of both inputs | computed live, pinned to nothing |
| Provenance | 99 rows, **21 measured, 78 derived**, every row tracing to a source page with a retrieval time and a content hash | none |

The guidance this repository is built against says it in one line — **do not build what the platform already has** — and the estate's own rule is that content exists once. A second vocabulary is not a second opinion, it is a thing that will disagree with the first one on a schedule.

**Recommendation.** Promote the pack at build time, do not fetch it at runtime. No page on this site opens a network connection and that is a build check, not an intention — so `tools/refs.py` grows a `promote` step that writes `data/abp/` with the pack's version, its retrieval time and its content hash, and `tools/check_site.py` gains a check that the promoted version is the one the pages claim. **That is exactly what abp.sgit.ai itself did** to the game map it took the vocabulary from, so the store would be copying a method rather than inventing one.

## Finding 4 — There is a traffic light on a document type that must carry none

Also mine, and the more embarrassing of the two, because I wrote a comment in `assets/lab.css` congratulating myself on it:

> `.is-excess` is red on purpose and is the only red on the page … If anything else on this page competes with it for attention, this stylesheet is wrong.

abp.sgit.ai's rule, stated on its home page, in its foundation document, in its data pack under `not_here.score`, and in the product strategy brief:

> There is no score, rating, traffic light, risk level or severity in this pack or anywhere on this site. A score is a verdict and the ABP describes without judging.

And the reason, which is not squeamishness: **the same ABP is dangerous in one deployment and harmless in another, and nothing about the document changed.** A policy cannot be dangerous; a deployment can. The score has a home, and it is the risk work above this, where the assets are known and a named professional signs — and the person who signs is not the person who sold, which is a rule this site already enforces by naming nobody beside a price.

**Recommendation.** Take the red and the `!` glyph out of `/lab/`. Excess becomes a count rendered in the same ink as every other count. What replaces the colour is the thing that actually carries the argument:

> **Your agent can do 16 things and you authorised 5.** Twelve of those sit at a barrier that is not a control.

That is a finding. `Twelve things in red` is a verdict, and it is the fastest way to make the document wrong in one of the two rooms it has to survive.

## Finding 5 — The configurator asks for a blank page, and the sale is a correction

The best sentence in the whole ABP corpus is the go-to-market, and it inverts what `/lab/` does:

> A draft ABP is derived from a deployment shape … The draft is handed over. **The correction is the elicitation.** A person who corrects the document has told you their mandate, told you where the draft's grant was wrong, and engaged with the product before paying for it.

Three properties make it right for next week, and all three hold for this site specifically:

1. **It is the only version producible in a room.** Every other form is a booking. This is five minutes over a printed draft, and **the printed draft is the card** — which this site already has four of.
2. **It is legally clean.** A draft about a deployment shape sends nothing anywhere, which satisfies the standing rule against sending an unrequested packet to a third party system. It asserts, and asks to be corrected.
3. **It respects the buyer's competence.** The mandate is theirs; the document asks them to state it rather than pretending to know it.

And the mechanic: **the draft should be wrong on purpose in one place.** Not misleading — *conservative*. It should state a grant the buyer recognises as too small, because the correction upward is the moment they realise the grant is larger than they thought.

**Recommendation.** Of the five prototypes, **the scenario view is the one that survives, and it needs inverting.** It currently offers five prebaked starts as a convenience. It should open with a *deliberately conservative draft* for the buyer's deployment shape, and the page's job becomes recording what they change. `data/brief.yml` already stores removals in `off` and reports them in `unknowns` — that half is right and was built for the wrong reason. Two additions:

- **Guess first.** abp.sgit.ai's home page asks the reader to write down, before scrolling, how many of 23 capabilities they think their deployment has and how many they asked for. The lab should ask the same two numbers before it shows anything, and keep both in the brief. Making somebody state a belief before they are told the answer is the games site's mechanic, and it is what turns a list into a finding.
- **Record the correction, not just the state.** The brief should carry `corrections: [{capability, from, to}]`, because a corrected draft is worth more than a filled-in form, and it is the evidence that the buyer engaged.

**The delta view stays as the working surface** — it is the one that explains the product without anyone narrating it — but it gains a third dimension it does not have today (below), and loses its colour (above). The interview, the ladder and the board can be retired once the pair is chosen; they were built to be chosen between and they have been.

## Finding 6 — The barrier is missing, and it is the column the money is in

`/lab/` computes excess and stops. The ABP records, for every capability in the grant, which of four things stands between the agent and it:

| | Barrier | What stands in the way | Is it a control |
|---|---|---|---|
| ● | none | nothing in the way | no |
| ◉ | expectation | a rule in prose, enforced by nobody | no |
| ◐ | setting | a switch the agent's own account can flip | no |
| ○ | boundary | enforced above the grant, out of the agent's reach | **yes** |

**The enforcer test: a control bounds a grant only if it is enforced by something the grant does not include.** A setting the agent's own account could change is not a control, because the grant includes the ability to remove the bound.

Two published facts sit under that and both belong on this site's pages: all four major model providers stated in their own 2026 words that an instruction at the prompt layer can be bypassed, and one provider reports that users approved roughly **93 per cent** of the permission prompts they were shown — the third row failing in the other direction.

Then the sentence that is worth more to this store than anything else in the corpus:

> **Unbounded excess is the only number on the label a buyer can move.** Every real control put in place shifts one capability into the fourth row and the number falls. **The gap between excess and unbounded excess is the business case for a control, and it contains no verdict.**

That is the mechanism the voice memo is describing when it says we are making the business case for why a security company is powerful. It is arithmetic over published rows, it names no vendor, it attaches no adjective to anybody, and it is therefore compatible with hard rule 8 — publish the record, never the verdict — which is the rule that currently keeps the whole dev-packs area closed.

## Finding 7 — A fourth buyer, and it needs no new offer

The voice memo names a segment this site does not have: **the company selling a control.** Their question is not *what did I grant it* — it is *what does my product actually move*.

The purchase is an ABP run twice against the same deployment shape, with and without their control, and the deliverable is the movement in unbounded excess. They buy tier three or tier four pointed in a different direction — **exactly the pattern the startup group already uses**, which is why it fits without touching the price list.

`check_buyer_groups` freezes the buyer set to three, so this is a code change and a ruling rather than a data edit. The current three, and the addition:

| Buyer | Arrives asking | Built for them |
|---|---|---|
| You run agents today | What did I actually grant it? | `t1` `t2` |
| You are backing a company | What would somebody who knows what they are doing find? | `t3` `t4` |
| You are a startup | What is diligence going to find before it finds it? | nothing — the reverse sale |
| **You sell a control** | **What does my product actually move?** | **nothing — the same tiers, run twice** |

Two groups out of four would then have nothing built for them. **That is a finding about the offer list and it should be visible rather than smoothed**, which is what the existing empty-group check already forces.

## Finding 8 — Tier two cites the wrong instrument for the buyer it is aimed at

The offer page and the ledger currently rest tier two on the European regulation, at 1,523 nodes with amendments applied. The product strategy brief is explicit that this is **not** the reason a United Kingdom buyer will cite:

- The omnibus regulation entered into force on 27 July 2026 and **deferred the high-risk deployer obligations to 2 December 2027**, and only for systems in the high-risk annex. An organisation whose agent is not high-risk has no duty under that article at all.
- The current, domestic reason is **consumer guidance published on 9 March 2026**: if an agent a business uses does something illegal, the business is responsible, and businesses **should be clear about what tasks an agent is allowed to perform, what data it can access, and what constraints apply**. That is the closest any regulator has come to describing an ABP by its contents.
- For the technical buyer: the industry's ten agentic application risks, published 9 December 2025, put **tool misuse and identity and privilege abuse at positions two and three**, with mitigations centred on least privilege, scoped credentials and enumerated tool catalogues. **An enumerated tool catalogue with a stated scope is the grant half of an ABP.**

**Recommendation.** Keep the graph and the price. Change what the page says the buyer is buying it *for*, and add the two citations to the ledger with their dates. The claim `graph-nodes` stays; a new claim carries the deferral, because a page that implies a duty applies today when it applies in fifteen months is the kind of thing this site exists not to do. Note the scope caveat honestly: the consumer guidance is consumer law, and whether it reaches agents that never touch a consumer is an open question in the source brief.

## Finding 9 — One word carries the entire demand story, and it may never appear here

**This is the ruling this brief most needs and cannot make.**

Hard rule 1 of this site's pack bars one word from every byte under `docs/` — not from copy, not from a heading, not from a filename, with no allowlist and no quotation carve-out. `tools/check_site.py` enforces it as `re.compile(r"insur", re.I)`, it is the check that closed the dev-packs area, and **11 of the 19 documents in this repository's own pack carry the word, one of them in its own title.**

The ABP's strongest demand evidence is built entirely out of that word. The only underwriter of autonomous agents by name requires, as a scoping input, a document listing capabilities, autonomy level, data access permissions, callable tools and deployment context — **which is an ABP**. The prohibitions an ABP enumerates are, precisely, the exclusion schedule of any instrument that later sits on top of it. Two of the eleven sources in this repository's manifest carry the word **in their own URL**.

Three options, and only one of them is cheap:

1. **Keep the rule absolute, and sell on the other three reasons.** The consumer guidance, the agentic top ten, and the sentence that needs no authority at all — *you know what you asked for, you do not know what it can do*. **The source brief itself states that the word appears on no page it describes**, so this is not a compromise invented here; it is the position the product already holds.
2. **Re-rule it for factual reference.** Expensive, and the rule's own reason argues against it harder now than before: a positioning phrase is a claim, but **a priced checkout is an offer, and an offer is what the regulated perimeter is about.** Naming an underwriter's requirement on a page that takes money is closer to that perimeter, not further from it.
3. **Put the demand story on a site that carries no price.** It is already there. abp.sgit.ai has no checkout and says it never will.

**Recommendation: option 1, with option 3 as the link.** The store sells the artefact; the library carries the argument; the word stays off every page here. The estate boundary the second strategy brief draws is the same one — **the estate sells the artefact the underwriter needs and never the instrument, because that is a regulated activity.**

*(This file sits in `admin/briefs/`, outside `docs/`, which is why it can discuss the rule at all. `refs/` is gitignored and outside the build tree for the same reason: two of the sources it caches carry the word, and nothing fetched can reach a page.)*

## Finding 10 — Three naming hazards, two of them mechanical

| Hazard | The rule | Can a script hold it |
|---|---|---|
| **`ADP`** is a large payroll processor's registered mark, and the source memos reach for it as a typo for ABP | **Nothing with that string on it may be printed** | **Yes.** A literal string check over `docs/`, and it should exist before anything is printed |
| **"the policy"** already denotes the instrument with bands, ceilings, a pool and a premium in this estate's own published demonstration | The behaviour policy is **always** the ABP or the behaviour policy, **never** the policy | **Partly.** Require that any page containing `ABP` also spells out *Agent Behaviour Policy* at least once |
| **Behaviour or behavior** | A wordmark decision, due before anything is printed | Once ruled, yes |

On the third: this site is written in United Kingdom English throughout and the estate's own site is `abp.sgit.ai` with *Behaviour* spelled out. **Recommendation: behaviour, and rule it before the cards go to print.**

## Finding 11 — The licence to operate collision now has a referent, which it never had

`check_naming_collision` requires *licence to operate* on this site's home page and bars *mandate to operate* from every page, and the ledger records the collision as **open since 8 September**. The product strategy brief supplies what neither phrase ever had, which is a thing to point at:

> **The organisation is the authority. The ABP is the instrument. The agent is the licensee.** It is self-issued and witnessed by us, which is how most assurance works.

That is consistent with the 3 September sense and it makes the 17 July phrase redundant — which is what the cheapest proposed resolution already recommended, and what this site's build already does by barring one and requiring the other. **So the store is already implementing the proposed resolution and has been for four days.**

**This is a proposal for a ruling and not a ruling.** But the home page's headline — *every agent needs a licence to operate* — has been a sentence with no referent since it was written, and there is now a document it names. That is the single cheapest messaging change available: one paragraph under the headline saying what the licence **is**.

## What Must Not Change

- **No score, no rating, no traffic light, no risk level, no severity** — anywhere in the pages, the data or the exported brief. The score lives on the risk product, where the assets are known and a named professional signs.
- **No verdict about any third party.** Where this brief quotes an incumbent's price — $2,199 one time, twenty or more editable templates, a thirty-minute onboarding call for the first twenty buyers, a thirty-day guarantee — that is the record, read from its own site on one day, with no adjective attached to it. The distinction the ABP claims is factual: a template cannot know what the buyer's agent can do.
- **The two payment rails never appear as a choice on one transaction.**
- **No page opens a network connection**, which is why the pack is promoted at build time and not fetched.
- **Nothing on this site is a compliance assessment and no page claims conformity**, which is word for word what abp.sgit.ai says an ABP is not.
- **The people who sell do not sign.** Both sites hold it; it is why the score and the artefact are two products and not two sections.

4. **The promoted pack is pinned.** The version, retrieval time and content hash in `data/abp/` match what every page claims, and the counts agree with the source. Same discipline as `check_lab_bands`.

## The Conference, In One Page

**The card is the printed draft.** Four codes already exist and redirect to `/d/<id>/`, the identifiers are stable, and the host is moveable — so nothing printed depends on anything that could change this week.

**The motion, in five minutes:**

1. Hand over a draft ABP for a deployment shape they recognise. Say it is probably wrong.
2. Ask the two numbers **before** showing the answer: how many of 23 capabilities do you think it has, and how many did you ask for?
3. Let them correct it. **The correction is the elicitation**, and the draft is conservative on purpose, so the correction goes upward.
4. The finding is the sentence, not the list: *your agent can do sixteen things, you authorised five, and twelve of those sit at a barrier that is not a control.*
5. The code on the card is tier one at £10. Tier three is the same document for a deployment nobody has profiled yet, which is a booking.

**What not to say at the table:** any score, any risk level, the word hard rule 1 bars, or *the policy* as a short form. And no adjective attached to any competitor's name.
